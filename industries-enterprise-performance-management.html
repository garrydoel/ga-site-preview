Build the Market Before the Project Exists

EPM is difficult to sell because the problem requires deep understanding.

Planning, forecasting, consolidation, reporting, workforce planning and supply-chain planning may sit under the same broad category, but they involve different buyers, different expertise and different commercial conversations.

At the same time, most finance teams are not actively buying an EPM platform today.

Growth depends on understanding where the buyer is in their journey, creating demand before a formal project exists and staying relevant until the buying window opens.

Let’s Talk

Why EPM Businesses Struggle to Scale

Select any that sound familiar. We’ll add them to your enquiry.

01

Long Buying Journeys

A finance team may recognise that its current planning process is failing but still be years away from acting.

An ERP implementation may need to finish first. An existing EPM contract may still have years to run. The annual budget may already be committed.

Commercial success therefore depends on more than identifying organisations showing intent today.

02

Deep Domain Knowledge

An EPM salesperson needs to understand enough about finance, technology, modelling and transformation to diagnose the problem properly.

FP&A is different from statutory consolidation. Financial planning is different from supply-chain planning. Workforce planning brings another set of data, language and business requirements.

Sales capability is much harder to build here than simply hiring somebody who can sell software.

03

More Competitors Than the Shortlist Suggests

Another EPM platform is only one competitor. The alternatives can include:

  • Spreadsheets
  • The incumbent platform
  • An ERP project taking priority
  • Delaying the decision
  • Doing nothing
  • The belief that generative AI may remove the need for specialist software
04

Capability Has to Grow With the Market

A consultancy strong in one EPM discipline can’t assume the same people can credibly sell and deliver another.

Moving into consolidation, supply chain or workforce planning requires the right expertise and enough market demand to justify building it.

AI Changes EPM But Doesn’t Remove the Need for Governed Planning

Finance increasingly expects quicker deployment, lower implementation overhead and more intelligent interaction with planning and reporting systems, and AI will change the entire experience.

But finance still needs to understand:

  • Where a number came from
  • How it was calculated
  • Which assumptions sit behind it
  • What data supports it
  • Whether the model can be governed and audited

The real opportunity is using AI to make governed EPM more useful, like finance getting faster and more intelligent planning without losing the audit trail and control.
