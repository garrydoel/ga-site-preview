Turn Expertise Into Commercial Momentum

Specialist firms often struggle to turn their knowledge and expertise into a clear market position and a consistent reason for the right buyers to engage.

Content gets produced, LinkedIn stays active, campaigns run and events happen. But activity only matters if it helps the market understand why it matters and why they should speak to you.

Godwin Austen connects positioning, content, campaigns and marketing execution to the commercial priorities of the business.

Talk to Us About Your Marketing

Marketing Should Make Sales Easier

For specialist consulting and technology firms, the job of marketing is making complex expertise easier to understand.

It should be educating buyers before they’re ready to speak to sales and keeping the business credible and visible across a long buying journey.

Done well, it also gives salespeople something useful to bring into the next conversation.

If none of those things are happening, producing more content is unlikely to solve the problem.

Where Marketing Starts to Lose Commercial Value

Select any that sound familiar. We’ll add them to your enquiry.

Unclear Positioning

The company can explain what it does technically, but buyers struggle to see why it matters commercially.

Generic Messaging

Different audiences receive the same message even though their problems and buying motivations differ.

Content Without Direction

Articles and posts are being created, but they are not tied to specific markets, accounts, buyer journeys or commercial priorities.

Campaigns Without Follow-Through

Marketing generates engagement but there is no consistent route from that engagement into sales nurture or account development.

Disconnected Sales & Marketing

Marketing sees clicks and engagement. Sales sees pipeline. Neither has a complete view of how the account is progressing.

Activity Without Measurement

The team can report what was published but struggles to show which activity is creating useful commercial movement.

Build Marketing Around the Buying Journey

A buyer who has never heard of you needs something different from an account already evaluating a project, and a technical stakeholder needs something different again from a CEO.

The same applies to timing. A company three years from an ERP migration shouldn’t get the same messaging as one actively assessing suppliers.

Performance improves when content and campaigns reflect that context, which is why understanding the industry matters to marketing as much as it does to sales.

The business is focused on markets where the buying journey is complex, credibility matters and demand often needs to be built long before an active opportunity exists.

Connect Marketing to the Rest of the Commercial Engine

Performance Marketing and Commercial Growth work closely together, but they have different jobs.

Performance Marketing builds the positioning, content, campaigns and market engagement that give buyers a reason to notice, understand and trust the business.

Commercial Growth takes that into the market through account development, outbound, sales conversations, pipeline management and commercial execution.

The two capabilities connect where marketing engagement becomes commercial activity, while other capabilities can be brought in to solve specific issues as needed.
